## What should you look for in a crypto exchange?

A crypto exchange can look attractive because it lists many coins or advertises low trading fees. Those details matter, but they are not enough to make a useful comparison.

### 1. Trading fees

The headline fee is only one part of the cost. You should check:

- Maker and taker fees
- Spot versus futures pricing
- Funding fees for perpetual contracts
- Withdrawal fees
- Card or payment-provider fees
- The spread on instant buy, sell, or convert services
- Whether your account qualifies for a higher fee tier

On OKX, the fee depends on the product, pair, execution type, account level, and region. A market order is usually charged at the taker rate because it matches existing orders. A limit order can still be charged as a taker if it executes immediately; it only receives maker treatment when it adds liquidity to the order book.

That distinction matters for active traders. A limit order is not automatically a maker order simply because it is a limit order.

## How much do OKX trades really cost?

Suppose you place a spot trade worth $1,000 and your account is charged a 0.35% taker fee. The trading fee would be approximately $3.50 before considering spread, slippage, conversion costs, or any separate payment and withdrawal charge.

A maker order at 0.20% would cost approximately $2.00 on the same notional amount if it is actually executed as a maker order.

The practical point is simple: **the fee percentage applies to the executed trade value, not just the cash you intended to use as margin.**

For futures, OKX calculates the trading fee from the position value. If you open a $20,000 position using $2,000 of margin at 10x leverage, the fee is calculated against the $20,000 position value. Leverage can therefore increase the size of the position and the associated trading cost even though your initial margin is smaller.

Perpetual futures also involve funding payments exchanged between long and short traders. Funding is separate from the trading fee. Depending on the funding rate and whether you are long or short, you may pay or receive funding. Liquidation fees and expiry settlement fees can also apply to derivatives.

For quick purchases, the cost may appear differently. OKX explains that Buy, Sell, and Convert flows may show a single quoted price rather than a separate maker or taker commission. The cost can be reflected in the quote or spread, so compare the amount you pay with the amount of crypto you receive instead of assuming that a visible “0% fee” means the transaction has no cost.

## Spot trading, instant buying, and Convert are different

New users often treat these three actions as interchangeable. They are not.

### Spot trading

Spot trading uses an order book. You select a trading pair, order type, price, and quantity. Your final execution depends on the available liquidity and how the order fills.

This route provides more control and is usually the better option for users who want to manage execution carefully.

### Buy and Sell

The Buy and Sell flow is designed for users who want a simpler path from cash to crypto. Available assets, currencies, payment methods, limits, and fees depend on the account market and region. OKX specifically advises users to rely on the options and limits displayed during checkout rather than old screenshots or fixed numbers from third-party articles.

### Convert

Convert is convenient when you want to exchange one eligible asset for another without using the full order-book interface. The quote is the key number to inspect. Check the amount you will receive, the expiration of the quote, and any displayed spread before confirming.

For a small occasional purchase, convenience may matter more than a fraction of a percentage point. For repeated or larger trades, the order book gives you more information to compare.

OKX supports perpetual futures, expiry futures, and options in eligible markets. Perpetual futures do not have a fixed expiry date and use funding rates to help keep the contract price aligned with the underlying market.

These products are designed for users who understand:

- Initial and maintenance margin
- Liquidation price
- Funding rates
- Mark price
- Contract size
- Realized and unrealized profit
- The difference between leverage and position size

A derivatives exchange interface can make opening a leveraged position look almost as easy as buying spot crypto. The risk is not equally simple. A small price move against a highly leveraged position can close it before the market has time to recover.

### Copy trading and trading bots

Copy trading and automated bots may help users execute predefined strategies, but they do not turn a losing strategy into a reliable one. Before using them, inspect:

- Historical drawdowns
- Trading pair selection
- Leverage settings
- Maximum position size
- Profit-sharing arrangements
- Whether results include fees and funding
- The conditions under which the strategy stops

Past performance is not a guarantee of future results, especially in a market where volatility can change quickly.

### Earn products

OKX offers several earning-related products, but they should not all be treated like a bank savings account. The risk depends on the specific product, underlying asset, lock-up period, counterparty structure, and whether the return is fixed or variable.

Read the product terms before depositing funds. A quoted yield is not the same thing as a guaranteed return, and the value of the underlying token can move substantially.

The OKX Web3 Wallet is separate from the centralized exchange account. It is designed for self-custody, DApps, decentralized exchanges, NFTs, cross-chain activity, and on-chain services. Assets are managed separately, so moving funds between the exchange and wallet may require an explicit transfer.

This distinction is important because the exchange account and the Web3 Wallet have different risks:
